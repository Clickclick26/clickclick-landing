Private appointments, on your own website

A video shopping appointment is a private video call between a client, anyone she brings with her, and the person who knows the piece. One to one, or a small curated group of up to eight. ClickClick Consult holds them three ways: by appointment, where a client takes a time that suits them; on request, where they ask to speak to someone and are seen shortly after; or as a private event, where you invite a few people to view a collection together. Everyone the client wants with them can join by link, and anything you show can be set aside for them before you say goodbye.

All the paperwork an appointment leaves

They are not hesitating over the price. They are hesitating over the piece.

Nobody commits to a piece of this kind having only seen it photographed.

A photograph will not tell her how the leather takes a mark, or whether the ivory is the one she has in her head. It will not show a collector the wear on a hallmark, or how the light sits in a stone. So she does the only sensible thing. She waits, asks a question, and waits again.

By the time the answer arrives, the moment has usually passed. Not because she found somewhere cheaper. Because she never quite got close enough to be sure, and nobody buys a piece they are unsure of.

You will recognise the questions, because you answer them every week:

  • Is that the colour in the photograph, or is it warmer than it looks
  • Will it need taking in, and can that be done in time
  • What is the condition really like, and what has been restored
  • Could I see it on, and could my mother see it too

None of that is answerable in writing. Twenty minutes with the person who knows the piece settles all four, and a client who has been shown something properly rarely needs persuading afterwards.

Three ways an appointment happens

All of them private, all of them in hours you choose. One client, or a few.

By appointment

A client takes a time that suits her and it arrives in your diary. You know who is coming and what she wants to see, so the pieces are ready before she joins.

On request

Someone already looking at a piece asks to speak to you. If you are free, you take it there and then. If you are not, they are offered a time rather than a form.

Private events

Choose a date, choose the pieces, and invite a handful of people to see them together. A first look at a collection, or a single lot shown to the three collectors who will care about it.

She will not come alone

Her mother, a friend whose taste she trusts, a sister three time zones away. Up to eight people on one appointment, each joining by link, none of them needing an account.

Your hours, and only yours

Nothing appears on your website outside the times you set. There is no button waiting to be pressed while you are at a fitting, and nothing rings when you are not there.

To the right person

Alterations to whoever does the alterations, provenance to whoever knows the provenance. If they cannot take it, it moves on rather than ringing them twice.

What you can do while she is with you

You begin knowing what she came to see, so you never open with “how can I help”.

Bring a piece up on her screen

Send it across mid-appointment and it appears in front of her, and in front of everyone she has brought with her. No link to follow, nothing to find in an inbox afterwards.

Set it aside for her

Anything she settles on goes into her own order while you are still speaking. She is never sent away to hunt for the thing she was just shown.

See what she came in on

The piece she was looking at, and anything already held for her, before the appointment begins. You are prepared without having to ask her to explain herself.

What you are left with afterwards

One line, written while it is fresh, and a record that means something next time.

A note for next time

What she liked, what she ruled out, what her mother thought. It is there when she comes back, which is usually a fortnight later with two more questions.

How it was left

Bought, thinking it over, not right for her, or only looking. Four honest endings and no vaguely positive one, so the record is worth reading a month later.

Who you reached, and who you did not

How many asked, how long they waited, how many were seen. That is what tells you whether to keep another afternoon free, and it is the only thing worth judging this on.

“There is only me, and I am usually with someone.”

Then this is built the right way round for you.

Nobody sits waiting at a screen. Appointments are the point: they sit in your diary the way a fitting does, and outside the hours you set there is nothing on your website to press. If you are with someone, it does not ring.

The number of people asking to see something properly is small. It is also, almost without exception, the people who are furthest along. An afternoon a week is a serious start, and if the diary fills you will know before we do.

  • Your hours, set by you, and invisible outside them
  • Sent to whoever knows that piece, not to whoever is nearest
  • Nothing rings twice for the same client
  • Working alone, from home, with no premises at all, is a supported way to use it and not a compromise

That last point is worth saying plainly. A good deal of the best work in this trade is done by one person with an eye, a workroom and no shop front. Consult does not assume you have a showroom, because for a great many of the people it was built for, the appointment is the showroom.

Who it is not for

Better said here than a season into working together.

Consult is built for pieces that need to be seen: bridal and occasion wear, antiques and period jewellery, designer boutiques, small ateliers, and the many people who sell beautiful things from home without a shop front. Few items, considered slowly, chosen with other people in the room.

It is the wrong tool for volume. If your work is many small orders that nobody deliberates over, an appointment diary is an expensive answer to a question your customers are not asking, and we would say so rather than take the work.

The biggest houses have done this for a century. They call it a private appointment, and they staff it with people whose whole job is to remember what a client wore last time. What none of them had until recently was a way to offer it to someone three hundred miles away who found you on a Tuesday evening. That part is new, and it does not need their budget.

Common questions about video shopping appointments

Is this the same as virtual personal shopping?

Yes, that is a fair description of it. Virtual personal shopping has meant a named associate, a client book and a salaried role, which only the biggest houses could staff. Consult gives a small or growing luxury business the same thing without the headcount: a client asks to see a piece, you take the appointment yourself, and everything you need to show it and set it aside is already in front of you.

How much does ClickClick Consult cost?

It is a monthly licence, priced per brand, and there is no charge per call. What it costs depends on how many of your staff need to answer calls and whether you also run live shopping shows on the same platform. Tell us what you sell and your average order value and you will get a figure on the first call, not after three meetings.

What happens if nobody is free when a client clicks talk to someone now?

The button only appears during the hours you set, and only when at least one of your experts is signed on. Outside those hours it is not on the page at all. When someone is on duty but busy, the client joins a queue and sees their position in it. If the call rings out without being answered, it stops ringing that person and moves on rather than ringing them twice for the same client. A button that rings out is worse for a retailer than no button, so the software is built to never leave one on the page.

Do I need to hire extra staff to offer video shopping appointments?

No. ClickClick Consult is designed to be answered by the people who already handle your hard questions, between other work. Nobody sits waiting at a screen. You set the opening hours, you choose who is on the rota, and calls route by topic so a question goes to the person who knows that category. Most retailers start with two hours a day and widen the hours only if the calls turn out to be worth taking.

Does the client need to download an app or turn their camera on?

No app. ClickClick Consult runs in the client’s browser on the retailer’s own website. The client’s camera stays off by default, so the video runs one way from your expert to them. That removes the most common reason a client backs out of a video call with a shop.

Can staff set items aside in the client’s own order during a video call?

Yes. Mid-call your expert pushes a product and it appears on the client’s own screen, with no link to click and no email to find later. Your expert can then add it to the client’s own order. She checks out herself, in her own session, on your own checkout. Nothing is bought on their behalf.

How do I know which sales came from a video shopping appointment?

Every call records the value of what was set aside during it, the page the client arrived from, and an outcome your expert marks at the end. The outcome options are bought on the call, going away to think, we were not right for them, and just browsing. There is no positive-sounding catch-all option, so staff cannot flatter the number.

Is a video shopping appointment the same as digital clienteling?

Not quite. Digital clienteling is an ongoing relationship programme: a named associate keeps a customer’s history, preferences and past purchases, and works that relationship over years. A video shopping appointment is a single conversation, and it works with a first-time visitor who has no history at all. Clienteling needs a programme and the staff to run one. A video shopping appointment needs one member of staff and an hour in the afternoon.

What is the difference between a video shopping appointment and live shopping?

A video shopping appointment is private: one client, or a small curated group of the people she brings and the people you invite, with one of your own staff. Live shopping is one to many: a host runs a scheduled show and hundreds of people watch and buy at once. An appointment needs no audience and no schedule, and it holds up to eight people, so a private event for a few invited clients is still an appointment. A show reaches far more people at once. Consult does the first, ClickClick Live does the second, and both run on the same platform.

Are video shopping appointments recorded, and is that GDPR compliant?

Calls are not recorded by default. What is kept is the written record: the products shown, the value set aside, the page the client arrived from, the outcome and a short note your expert writes. If you choose to turn recording on, you are responsible for telling clients before the call starts and for having a lawful basis to do it. We will help you set that up, but we will not tell you that switching a feature on makes you compliant.
