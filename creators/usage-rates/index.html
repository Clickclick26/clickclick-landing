What to charge when a brand runs your video as an ad

Filming and usage are two different things. You get paid once to make the video. You get paid again for where it runs and how long it runs there. Nearly everyone finds this out after the fact, and the person on the other end of the email already knew.

The bit nobody explains

Your day rate covers the making. Turning up, filming, editing, revisions. That is the work and it ends when you send the file.

Usage is separate. It is the brand buying the right to put your face and your video somewhere, for a set length of time. A video sitting on their own Instagram grid is one thing. The same video running as a paid advert to two million people for a year is a different thing entirely, and it is worth a lot more to them.

If there is nothing in writing, the safe assumption is the brand got the paid ad for free. That is how a video you made last year is still selling for someone while you were paid once.

The three questions that set the price

Before you quote anything, you need these three answers. They are the whole job.

  1. Where? Their own social accounts only, or paid ads too? Paid ads cost more. Website, email, shop screens, TV, billboards all cost more again.
  2. How long? Three months, six, twelve? Forever is not a length of time and should never be agreed to.
  3. Where in the world? One country, or everywhere?

A brand that cannot answer these has not thought about it yet. That is fine, and it is also the moment you get to set the terms.

What usage is worth

Usage is normally priced as a percentage on top of the fee for making the video. These are the ranges UK creators work within. They are a starting point for working yours out, not a fixed price list.

Worked example. You charge £400 to make a video. They want to run it as a paid ad in the UK for six months. £400 plus 50% usage is £600. Same filming, same day, £200 more, because you asked.

Whitelisting is not the same thing

Whitelisting, sometimes called Spark Ads or a Partnership Ad, is when the brand runs ads from your account rather than theirs. Your name, your handle, your followers see it. That is a bigger ask than normal usage, because it puts their advert in your face to your own audience.

Charge for it separately, and keep it short. Thirty days at a time is normal, and it gives you a way out if the campaign turns out to be something you would rather not be the face of.

Exclusivity is the one that costs you most

Exclusivity means you cannot work with anyone similar for a set period. It is not usage, it is you turning down future work, and it should be paid for on its own.

Watch for how wide it is written. "No other drinks brands" is one thing. "No other food or drink" quietly rules out most of your year. Ask for it in writing, narrow it to direct competitors, and put an end date on it.

What to actually say

You do not need to be tough about this. You need to be normal about it. Usage is an ordinary part of how this work is priced, and saying so calmly is most of it.

"Happy to do it. Quick one before I quote: is this for your own channels, or are you planning to run it as a paid ad as well? And roughly how long for? The rate changes depending, so I want to get it right first time."

When they come back asking for a year of paid ads:

"That works. So that is £400 for the video, plus usage for paid ads in the UK for twelve months, which is £300. £700 in total. I will put it on the invoice as two lines so it is clear what covers what."

When they say the budget will not stretch:

"No problem at all. We can drop it to six months and that brings it to £600, or keep it to your own channels at £400. Whichever suits the budget."

That last one matters more than the others. You are not lowering your price. You are lowering what they get for it. Those are very different things, and doing it this way keeps your rate intact for the next brand.

Put it on the invoice as two lines

Not one line saying "content". Two lines, so there is no argument later:

  • Production, one video: £400
  • Usage, paid social, UK, 12 months from 1 October 2026: £300

The date is the important part. It means the usage has an end, and when it ends you have a reason to go back to them. Brands that like the video will renew, and that renewal is money you did no new work for.

If you have already been caught by this

Almost everyone has. If a video of yours has been running as an ad with nothing in writing, you have not lost anything permanently. Nothing stops you saying at the next job that your rate now separates filming from usage, and pricing it properly from there.

It is worth saying plainly: this happens because nobody sat you down and explained it, not because you did anything wrong.
