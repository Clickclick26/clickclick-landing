What is live commerce?

Live commerce is selling products through live video, where viewers can watch a host demonstrate items and buy them in real time without leaving the stream. It is also called live shopping.

The short version

A live commerce broadcast has three parts working together: a live stream, a shoppable product list pinned to that stream, and checkout that happens in place. A host presents products, answers questions in the chat, and often runs time-limited offers. Viewers tap a product and buy without opening a new tab.

It started on Chinese platforms like Taobao Live, then became a mainstream channel in the West through TikTok Shop and Amazon Live. For consumer brands it sits between content and retail: the entertainment of a live show with the conversion of a product page.

How live commerce works, step by step

  1. Plan the show. Choose products that demonstrate well on camera, write a run of show, and set any launch or bundle offers.
  2. Connect the catalogue. Live commerce software links the products, prices and stock to the broadcast so what is on screen is buyable and accurate.
  3. Go live with a host. A presenter, whether in-house talent, a creator or an influencer, demos products and responds to the chat in real time.
  4. Sell in the stream. Viewers add to basket or check out without leaving the video. Offers and stock update live.
  5. Keep selling afterwards. The recording is cut into shoppable clips that continue to convert as on-demand content.
  6. Measure and repeat. Sales are attributed by stream, product and host, so the next show is better than the last.

Live commerce vs traditional e-commerce

A normal product page is passive: the shopper reads specs and looks at photos alone. Live commerce adds a person, a demonstration, real-time answers and a reason to buy now. That tends to lift conversion and average order value during the broadcast, and it produces content and first-party insight you can reuse. The trade-off is that it takes planning, a host and the software to run it.

Live commerce vs social commerce

Social commerce is the umbrella term for buying that happens inside social platforms, including static shoppable posts and product tags. Live commerce is the live-video form of it. Every live commerce stream is social commerce, but not all social commerce is live.

Where live commerce happens

  • TikTok Shop. Live shopping is native to the app, with a large built-in discovery audience.
  • Amazon Live. Live video attached to product listings and storefronts.
  • Owned channels. A brand's own website or app, where it keeps the full customer relationship and margin.
  • Other platforms. Instagram, YouTube and some retail media networks run live shopping formats too.

Why it works

Live commerce compresses the path from interest to purchase. Seeing a product used answers the questions that usually stall a sale, the chat handles the rest, and a live offer gives a reason not to postpone. It also builds an audience that returns for the next show.

How to get started

Begin small: a handful of products that show well on video, a host who knows them, and software that ties the stream to your catalogue and checkout. Run a short recurring show rather than a one-off, track results by stream and creator, and put more behind whatever converts.

Live commerce FAQ

What is live commerce in simple terms?

Live commerce is a live video broadcast where a host shows products and viewers can buy them straight away, without leaving the stream. It combines a live stream, a shoppable product list and checkout in one experience. It is also called live shopping.

What is the difference between live commerce and social commerce?

Social commerce is any buying that happens inside a social platform, including static posts and product tags. Live commerce is the live-video form of it, with real-time hosting, questions and offers during a broadcast. Live commerce is one part of social commerce.

Where does live commerce happen?

Mostly on TikTok Shop and Amazon Live, and increasingly on brands’ own websites and apps. Instagram, YouTube and retail media networks also run live shopping formats. The best channel depends on where a brand’s customers already are and the margin each channel leaves.

How do brands get started with live commerce?
